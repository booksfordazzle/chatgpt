Animal fats and mastic: history, sources and modern skincare

By PRIMAL CORE · Updated 19 November 2026

The historical inspiration behind PRIMAL CORE, and the distinction between tradition, ingredient research and assessment of a modern cosmetic.

Tradition can inspire ingredient choices and simpler routines. It does not by itself establish the safety or effectiveness of a modern cosmetic.

Historical use and modern evidence

We do not attribute a specific tallow balm recipe to Hippocrates, or modern skin-regeneration claims to Dioscorides, without a verified primary citation. A historical account of an ointment is not a clinical study of a present-day formula.

Animal fats, resins and waxes also need to be identified precisely. Mastic resin, mastic oil and an extract are not interchangeable materials.

What is documented about the Chios tradition?

UNESCO has inscribed the know-how of cultivating mastic on Chios on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity. This recognizes a cultural practice, not the effectiveness of a cosmetic.

What does the EMA monograph cover?

The European Medicines Agency publishes a monograph on Pistacia lentiscus L. resin in the context of traditional herbal medicinal products. It does not approve PRIMAL CORE or automatically transfer indications for the resin to mastic oil or a face balm.

Where is PRIMAL CORE today?

We are developing a men's face balm centered on beef tallow and Chios mastic oil. Tallow does not have the same composition as human sebum. Ingredient origin does not guarantee the performance, stability or suitability of the finished product.

We are not presenting tests demonstrating immediate post-shave soothing, wound healing, after-sun protection or non-greasy absorption for PRIMAL CORE. Its final formula, directions and substantiated claims will be finalized after the necessary checks.
